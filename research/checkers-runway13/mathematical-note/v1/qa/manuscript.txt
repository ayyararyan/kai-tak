Certiﬁed Local Optimality in a Constrained
Landing Model
Aryan Ayyar
A smooth airborne trajectory must satisfy force and control-rate limits
at every time, clear an assumed occupied geometry, and connect to
a feasible touchdown and stopping motion. We study this problem
in a twelve-parameter polynomial family with a ﬁxed hybrid contact
and braking law. For a declared constant wind, we prove existence
of an exact active solution with primary normalized margin near
0.1523607643018617. An outward-rounded Krawczyk calculation iso-
lates a unique root of nine equations, coupling ﬁve ﬂare variables, the
objective value and three interior event times. A validated positive
simplex of six active gradients supplies a strict ﬁrst-order max-min
sufﬁciency argument, rather than only necessary stationarity conditions.
Stationary-event inequalities and a complete interval time cover establish
feasibility of the connected landing. Dependence of the active margins
on the ﬂare variables alone transfers the result to non-strict local primary
optimality in the full family. Analytical stopping and coupled energy–
thrust inequalities give a separate, unmatched global-family upper bound
of 0.181970215. We also establish continuous primary retention for a
delivered secondary trajectory and certiﬁed feasibility of small known-
wind parameter domains. The conclusions concern the explicit reduced
model and conservative assumed geometry; global primary optimality,
secondary-effort optimality and an operational feedback landing policy
remain open.

1. Introduction
A landing is a constrained connection between two different dynamical
regimes. During ﬂight, the shape and timing of the trajectory determine lift,
thrust, bank and their rates. At touchdown, geometric contact replaces airborne
motion; stopping then consumes the remaining runway. A trajectory can
respect each endpoint requirement yet fail between endpoints, and a feasible
numerical construction need not be locally optimal. The mathematical problem
is to establish both statements for the same precisely deﬁned object.
This note considers the assumption-conditional model A1 developed
alongside Checkers with Runway 13 (Ayyar 2026a,b). Its variables describe
a seventh-degree approach joined to a sixth-degree ﬂare, followed by ideal
contact, a ﬁxed delay and constant-deceleration ground roll. The primary
objective is the smallest normalized operating margin over the complete
trajectory. Geometry is imposed as a hard constraint. The optimization is
ﬁnite-dimensional, but its inequality system is indexed by continuous time.
The principal result concerns ﬁve ﬂare parameters. Six limiting margins
meet at a common value: stopping, three thrust-slew events, maximum load
and minimum thrust. The interior event times are unknowns, so equal margins
and stationarity deﬁne nine equations. A veriﬁed root is only the ﬁrst step.
We prove that the active gradients form a simplex containing zero in its strict
interior. Consequently every nonzero small ﬂare perturbation decreases at least
one necessary margin to ﬁrst order. A uniform Taylor estimate makes this a
strict local sufﬁciency theorem. Continuous-time veriﬁcation then connects
this upper-support argument to an actually feasible landing.
The approach has seven additional parameters. The six necessary active
margins do not depend on them: the gate state is ﬁxed and their evaluation
is entirely within the ﬂare or ground roll. This separation proves a non-strict
local optimum of the twelve-parameter family. It neither identiﬁes a unique
approach nor permits arbitrary variations outside that family. An independent
family-wide exclusion gives the bracket

8. Discussion and open mathematical problems
The positive result is a certiﬁed connection between an exact local optimizer
and a complete feasible hybrid trajectory. Root isolation identiﬁes an actual
solution of the active equations. The gradient simplex proves strict local
primary optimality in ﬁve ﬂare coordinates. Stationary-time arguments and
interval coverage establish feasibility, including the approach, occupied
geometry and ideal contact law. The dependence structure then transfers the
upper-support argument to a non-strict local maximum in twelve parameters.
The remaining global gap is concrete. The achieved lower endpoint in
(1) and the family exclusion differ by approximately 0.0296094506981383.
Closing that gap requires either a better feasible construction or a stronger
exclusion using constraints discarded by the energy relaxation. The six active
ﬂare constraints suggest a lower-dimensional global search, but local sharpness
does not exclude another branch. Nor does the terminal relaxation identify the
controls needed to attain its scalar bound.
The secondary problem is also distinct. Continuous primary retention has
been certiﬁed, and numerical effort minimizations have been delivered, but
no inequality proves their effort optimality. A complete condition map would
require validated continuation of active branches, treatment of changing active
sets and geometric disjunctions, and either constructions or exclusions for
the unresolved wind and urban cases. The three small wind neighborhoods
establish only the explicitly parameterized feasible family.
The mathematical and physical domains should be kept separate. This
reduced model omits calibrated aerodynamic uncertainty, six-degree-of-
freedom attitude dynamics, load-rated contact, causal gust response and
go-around viability. Its scene completes missing obstacle coverage through
explicit assumptions. Those limitations do not negate the local theorem; they
identify the object to which it applies. A historical or operational claim would
require a different evidential and modelling argument.

Appendix E.
Continuous-time veriﬁcation algorithm
The veriﬁer has two logically separate inputs: exact polynomial/physical data
and a ﬁnite list of proved active neighborhoods. Its procedure is:
1. Enclose the root parameters and construct interval Hermite coefﬁcients,
with exact endpoint identities retained symbolically.
2. Split [0,1] at every special-event neighborhood endpoint. Treat approach
and ﬂare as separate closed domains.
3. On each cell, enclose position through jerk, inverse forces, exact rates,
corridor distance and all geometric predicates. Remove only a margin
whose entire cell lies in its proved neighborhood.
4. Accept only after all remaining margin lower endpoints exceed 𝜂, and all
hard geometry lower endpoints are nonnegative. Otherwise bisect, or return
unresolved at the depth limit.
5. Check that the accepted closed cells cover both phases without gaps; check
terminal margins, factored gear contact and the whole ground segment.
The maximum accepted subdivision depth in the local certiﬁcate is 12. Cells
are enclosures of all real times in their domains, not quadrature nodes.
The exact numerical value of a selected subdivision endpoint is its stored
decimal representation, consistently used in both event and ordinary interval
evaluations.
The disjunctive geometry extension encloses maxima and minima by taking
the corresponding extrema of endpoint bounds. It is inclusion-preserving but
can be pessimistic. The family dependence is likewise enclosed, not discarded:
an interval hull may include combinations no actual parameter realizes, which
can make certiﬁcation harder but cannot make a false trajectory feasible.
For the wind-family check, ﬁrst-order parameter differentiation reduces that
dependency inﬂation before the force enclosure.
