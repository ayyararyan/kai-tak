\abstract{A smooth airborne trajectory must satisfy force and control-rate limits at every time, clear an assumed occupied geometry, and connect to a feasible touchdown and stopping motion. We study this problem in a twelve-parameter polynomial family with a fixed hybrid contact and braking law. For a declared constant wind, we prove existence of an exact active solution with primary normalized margin near $0.1523607643018617$. An outward-rounded Krawczyk calculation isolates a unique root of nine equations, coupling five flare variables, the objective value and three interior event times. A validated positive simplex of six active gradients supplies a strict first-order max-min sufficiency argument, rather than only necessary stationarity conditions. Stationary-event inequalities and a complete interval time cover establish feasibility of the connected landing. Dependence of the active margins on the flare variables alone transfers the result to non-strict local primary optimality in the full family. Analytical stopping and coupled energy--thrust inequalities give a separate, unmatched global-family upper bound of $0.181970215$. We also establish continuous primary retention for a delivered secondary trajectory and certified feasibility of small known-wind parameter domains. The conclusions concern the explicit reduced model and conservative assumed geometry; global primary optimality, secondary-effort optimality and an operational feedback landing policy remain open.}
