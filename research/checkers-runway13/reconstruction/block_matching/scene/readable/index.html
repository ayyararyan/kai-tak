Reading the reconstruction

The original grey 3D view was hard to interpret. Start with the labelled map, then compare the recovered surface in the same orientation.

This is a partial reconstruction of a small city area, not a finished historical city model. Missing surface patches are unknown, not empty ground.

1. Find your bearings

North is up. This October 1989 map covers a small Kowloon City / Tung Tau area, not the whole approach. Blue labels locate six inspected mapped forms; they do not establish each building’s state in 1993.

2. See what was recovered

Same area, same orientation, reconstructed from 2 November 1993 photographs. Purple means lower relative elevation; yellow means higher. The scale runs from −10 to 60 metres; pale blue and orange mark values beyond its ends. Grey gaps have no mesh coverage. Blue outlines come from the earlier map, not from identified 1993 roof boundaries.

These elevations are relative to a fitted local plane, not sea level or each building’s ground. Calibration is conditional, and most points lie outside local map controls. Colour is a display aid, not an added measurement.

3. Inspect one small area

A: market north roof feature, approximately 10.77 m above the reference plane. B: south rooftop service structure, approximately 19.20 m above that plane.

Both highlighted points lie outside the map-control hull. These are conditional estimates, not calibrated height bounds. Broken edges and holes are unresolved observations, not real building shapes. The colour scale is the same as in the preceding view.
